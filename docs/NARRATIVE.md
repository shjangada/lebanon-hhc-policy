# Narrative: the health rider (for the dashboard flow, memo and pitch)

**One line.** The money is already in Beqaa's fields; health is not attached to it. A small rider on projects that are already funded, run by Lebanese organisations, can turn environmental recovery into accessible care, with control moving to Lebanese hands as capacity is shown.

The Beqaa Valley here means five districts (Zahle, West Bekaa, Rachaya, Baalbek, Hermel), a physical region and not the Bekaa governorate alone. Every number carries a source id (`data/registry/sources.csv`) or an assumption label; ranges and status are in `docs/EVIDENCE_TABLE.md`.

## Grapevine framing (the challenge's own hook: the Barake et al. grapevine-inspired framework)
**Detect** (risk and need scores: the Need Score, the 2024 water-borne-disease risk list, the start-site scorer) **-> adapt** (the escrow handover with pre-agreed thresholds, capacity-aware routing of red flags, the visit schedule) **-> connect** (the care ladder: prevent -> CHW care in settlements -> remote triage -> outreach visit -> red flag to the centre with spare capacity). One slide, one line: *money and visits follow measured need, adapt to capacity, and connect every finding to a clinic.*

## The five beats
1. **Why Beqaa.** About 861,000 people (LRP 2026) in 222 cadasters; 1,030 conflict incidents recorded between Oct 2023 and Jul 2025 (LRP; the 2026 war is not in this series); 65 primary-care centres and 29 hospitals in the MoPH list; moderate or severe food insecurity 44.3% nationally in 2024 (FAO via World Bank, national only). Older figures are labelled old (for example 42% of cultivated area, c. 2000, reported). Soil results exist for 5 villages (rung a). Government pays 13.4% of health spending (SC02).
2. **Where the money goes today.** 37 inventoried projects (water, agriculture, livelihoods); none found that tests soil or screens people for exposure (desk search, to confirm). The LHF's share to national NGOs is 38% to 47% in 2023-2026, but only 0% / 5.3% / 0% (2024-2026) for health projects whose title names Beqaa (a proxy; projects not naming Beqaa are missed). Globally 4.3% of funding went directly to local actors in 2025 against a 25% pledge (ALNAP, S45). FTS health totals look incomplete and are not quoted.
3. **The gap.** Water is the lead evidence: 72% of tested settlement water samples in Baalbek-Hermel were faecally contaminated (Jul 2026), about 70% of national wastewater is discharged untreated (CDR/MoEW, reported), nitrates have been measured in Beqaa groundwater (2007-09 and 2023 studies, to verify), and reported hepatitis A and typhoid are a steady burden (hepatitis A in the Bekaa file: 690, 779, 270 and 67 to August in 2023-2026; late-2025 months are reporting lag). Soil is a second, weaker line that the pilot tests: chromium 110-230 mg/kg in four villages and lead 230 mg/kg in Bouday are environmental findings (rung a) with a plausible crop and work route (rung b); the hazard quotient runs from 0.27 to 280 depending on the Cr(VI) share, which nobody has measured, and **no health effect is shown: (c) is not established**. Farm workers, including displaced Syrians without status, are the most exposed and the least counted.
4. **The proposal.** A **rider** (testing, trigger, screening and referral at the nearest MoPH-list centre, worker protection), delivered through the LHF window and optionally on funded host projects, costing about $3,800 to $4,200 each (0.5% to 0.9% of the budget where known; sample counts are assumptions). Run by a local consortium led by an existing primary-care operator (candidate: Amel, to verify), with women-led and farming cooperatives on the committee; financed through a ring-fenced Beqaa window within the Lebanon Humanitarian Fund (proposed; the 2026 second reserve allocation of USD 15M already funds WASH and Health with frontline partners, registry F004). Adding the rider to a host project's own safeguard or OHS line is an optional cost saving, not a requirement: no host project could be confirmed. Control moves INGO to national NGO to union of municipalities to MoPH as pre-agreed thresholds are met (Gavi-style); at national-anchor capacity growth of +0.03 a year the union rung is reached about 2029 (scenario).
5. **Why it holds up, and what could break it.** Small cost per rider; break-evens are explicit (below); the pilot measures what is unknown (Cr(VI) share, referral completion, posts filled, capacity, reach of unregistered workers). Outside our control: renewed conflict (scenario S1), funding cuts (S2), staff loss (S4), and funders refusing scope creep.

## How care works today, and where to start (added with the pilot-flow models)
Primary care is open to all nationalities (MoPH network; LPSP fee $2 GP / $3.5 specialist), but three links are missing: no trigger from a soil or water finding to a health action, no tracking of referral completion, and no hospital payer found for unregistered Syrians since UNHCR stopped hospital support on 1 Dec 2025 (MoPH covers 100% for uninsured Lebanese at government hospitals in the war period). Outreach exists (Amel: 14 mobile units reaching 65 sites; MSF centres in Hermel and Arsal and mobile teams) but no visit frequency is published. Reported hepatitis A in the Bekaa file was 690 (2023), 779 (2024), 270 (2025) and 67 (2026 to August, partial; late-2025 months are reporting lag); 72% of tested settlement water samples in Baalbek-Hermel were faecally contaminated (Jul 2026). The start-site scorer (`start_site.py`) ranks cadasters on soil results, a funded host project, centre access, settlements (2016, old), an outreach operator, reported waterborne disease and need: on current data the scorer's first pick is Saadnayel in Zahle, followed by cadasters in Baalbek and Zahle, and a Hermel cadaster ranks 20th; **the pilot starts in the top-ranked cluster because the scorer picked it**. Rank stability: no single cadaster is stable under random weights (Saadnayel first in 21% of 1,000 draws), and Zahle and Baalbek are the two candidate districts (first place goes to Baalbek 42%, Zahle 29%, West Bekaa 24%, Hermel 5% of random draws; Zahle 80% and Baalbek 20% when each weight is perturbed by 25%): choose a district and a cluster, not one cadaster. Bouday (rank 17) and Chmestar (Chmistar, rank 81), the CNRS-L soil villages, are a second wave: they rank lower on centre access and settlement population but have soil data, and REACH HSM 2025 (reported) covers Bouday. A prevention package avoids few REPORTED cases (thousands of dollars each): the case for it rests on diarrhoea and severity not counted in the notified data, and on the pilot.

## Options compared
| Option | What it is | Impact | Cost | Feasibility | Equity | Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Status quo | agriculture, water and health funded in silos, INGO-led | none on exposure: no trigger from findings to care | no new cost | high (it is what happens) | low: workers outside registers are missed; control stays foreign | low |
| B. New standalone health programme | separate donor screening programme | could screen more people | highest and unsourced; new money while the LRP is 39% funded and the Flash Appeal 54% | low: new money, a parallel system, F1 fails | medium if designed well | high |
| **C. Riders + local consortium + escrow** | testing and referral attached to funded projects, Lebanese-led | targeted at places with a funded project, a soil finding and high need; effect sizes are placeholders except CHW (0 to +16.9 points on ANC, non-Lebanon) | about $4k per rider; CHW $12-54k per 10 CHW-years (sourced) | medium-high: no new public money, existing institutions; depends on funder eligibility and on a pilot | highest by design (all workers regardless of status), but unproven: between-group gaps are unobservable | high |

Scoring is qualitative judgement from the model outputs and sourced costs, not a computed score. **What would flip it:** with community priorities as modelled the community arm loses to the status quo at every tested value; in the best case it wins only if capture < 5.6% or local fit > 1.48 (`breakeven.csv`). Option C's allocation rule is therefore a pilot hypothesis, while the rider itself does not depend on it.

## Collateral-damage check (every recommendation)
- **Money pegged to landowners misses Syrian workers:** require services and protection for all workers regardless of status; track the share of services used by unregistered workers (anonymous community-ID tally); unobservable from public data today.
- **Stigma and market harm from test results:** results go to households and the clinic first; no public release by village without consent.
- **Overloading centres:** the rider is flagged when it adds more than 10% of a centre's assumed yearly capacity.
- **Capture and elite control:** committee seats for women (30%) and workers (10%), published quarterly spend, third-party spot checks (escrow rules).
- **Dependency and parallel systems:** the rider uses existing MoPH-list centres and existing project channels; no new clinics; control moves to Lebanese entities by pre-agreed thresholds.
- **Funder refusal:** use the projects' own safeguard or OHS lines where allowed; otherwise the ring-fenced window.
- **Indicator gaming:** referral completion and posts filled are self-reported: sampled audits before any tranche.
- **Scope:** air pollution and generators are real but outside a land and water rider (limitation).

## Pitch line
About 70% of Lebanon's wastewater is discharged untreated (CDR/MoEW, reported), and 72% of tested water samples in Baalbek-Hermel's tented settlements were faecally contaminated in July 2026. Reported hepatitis A and typhoid persist across the five districts. Yet none of 37 funded projects we found in Beqaa tests the water people drink or links what it finds to a clinic. Soil chromium in four villages is a finding we propose to test, not a harm we can claim.
