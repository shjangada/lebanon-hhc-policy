# Decision-maker and route

**Who decides.** The Lebanon Humanitarian Fund (LHF) allocates money from its pooled fund; the Humanitarian Coordinator decides on the advice of the LHF Advisory Board and the Humanitarian Financing Unit (general CBPF governance: **verify in the LHF operational manual, which could not be opened**). **MoPH approves** the clinical side: the referral endpoint (MoPH-list primary-care centres), standards and reporting. The consortium lead (a primary-care operator, candidate Amel, to verify) implements.

**Why the LHF, and why alongside LEAP/CDR rather than instead of it.**
- The LHF is already the channel that funds national NGOs and funds WASH and Health: its 2026 second reserve allocation was USD 15M with WASH and Health among the priority sectors (registry F004, ReliefWeb); national NGOs held 38% to 47% of LHF project budgets in 2023-2026. A small ring-fenced Beqaa window needs a decision, not a new vehicle (feasibility filter F1: no new public money).
- LEAP (World Bank, USD 250M via the CDR; registry F008) is a government-channel loan with its own procurement and safeguard rules and is national: it fits the later rungs of the ladder (municipal union -> MoPH), when the thresholds are met, not a 12-month pilot.
- The pilot runs through the LHF and produces the evidence (referral completion, capacity, reporting) that a government-channel scale-up needs.

**What is not confirmed:** whether the LHF would accept a Beqaa window or a budget revision to add testing and referral to an approved project (the CBPF revision rules and the LHF manual could not be opened); no host project could be confirmed, so a host add-on is an optional cost saving, not a requirement.
