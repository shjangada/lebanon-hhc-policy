# WHO GDWQ: minimum sampling frequency for setting a testing cadence

**Source:** WHO *Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality*, 4th ed. incorporating the 1st addendum, Chapter 4 (Water safety plans), Table 4.4, p. 67.
https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/wash-documents/water-safety-and-quality/dwq-guidelines-4/gdwq4-with-add1-chap4.pdf
(The table is carried into the later editions with the 2nd addenda (2022) and 3rd addenda; I did not re-check those editions in this session.)

## Table 4.4: Recommended minimum sample numbers for faecal indicator testing in distribution systems

| Type of water supply and population served | Total number of samples per year |
|---|---|
| Point sources | Sample every source progressively, over a cycle of 3–5 years at most |
| Piped supply, < 5,000 | 12 |
| 5,000 – 100,000 | 12 per 5,000 population |
| > 100,000 – 500,000 | 12 per 10,000 population, plus 120 additional samples |
| > 500,000 | 12 per 50,000 population, plus 600 additional samples |

- **Footnote (p. 67):** chlorine, turbidity and pH should be tested more often, as part of operational and verification monitoring.
- **§4.3.4 (p. 67):** microbial samples, together with chlorine residual, pH and turbidity, are usually taken frequently from dispersed sites. Stratified random sampling works well.
- **§4.3.5 (p. 68), community-managed supplies:** a rolling programme of surveillance visits, so that each supply is visited once every 3–5 years. Each visit covers E. coli, chlorine, turbidity, pH and a sanitary inspection.
- **Operational monitoring (pp. 62–63):** a chlorine residual that suddenly disappears can mean contamination has got in. Intervals can run from online chlorine control to quarterly checks.
- **Cross-check:** Australian Drinking Water Guidelines Table 9.4 is described as "based on WHO recommendations (WHO 2008)". Its minimums are 1 sample per week for zones under 5,000, plus 1 per month for each extra 5,000 people between 5,000 and 100,000. https://guidelines.nhmrc.gov.au/australian-drinking-water-guidelines/part-3/chapter-9/9.5-verification-of-drinking-water-quality/9.5.2-drinking-water-quality-monitoring

## Applying this to Bekaa / Baalbek-Hermel informal settlements (worked arithmetic only)

ITS water comes mostly by truck or from point sources, not from piped networks. Table 4.4 is therefore a **floor for verification**, not an emergency standard.

- Population served under 5,000 → 12 E. coli samples a year (1 a month).
- 50,000 people on one trucking scheme → 12 × (50,000 / 5,000) = 120 a year (10 a month).
- 112,629 people (ITS population reached nationally by WaSH, Jan–Sep 2026) treated as one system → 12 × (112,629 / 10,000) + 120 ≈ 135 + 120 = 255 a year (about 21 a month).
- Free residual chlorine should be tested **more often** than E. coli (Table 4.4 footnote). GDWQ gives no fixed number for this.
- Context: in July 2026, 72% of tested samples in Baalbek-Hermel ITS showed faecal contamination (UNICEF SitRep 5, citing an ACF assessment). That argues for going above the minimum.

**Not verified in this session:** Sphere or WASH-sector emergency FRC targets (for example 0.2–0.5 mg/L at the point of delivery), and the Lebanon WaSH sector's own testing SOP. Confirm these before citing them.
